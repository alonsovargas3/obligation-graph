## Systematic patterns

1. **Duration is not an undated deadline event.** Reference 12, 13, 17, 21 and 23 concern continuous compliance, maintenance or insurance. The frozen grammar specifically routes `during`/`throughout` without a recognized construction to `anchor_not_found`. Calling these `anchor_without_date` suggests an event-date resolution failure that is not present. Do not remedy this by inventing a lease-end deadline or calling the quotes devoid of timing language.

2. **Semantic reference labels and the closed grammar are not fully aligned.** `When changes are made`, ordinal `thirtieth (30th)`, daily reporting, monthly holdover rent, alternative end events and maturity-to-payment intervals are meaningful source timing. Their grammar gaps should be disclosed or resolved explicitly rather than silently changing correct semantic labels to whatever a limited parser happens to produce. Conversely, a `gt` label for `Once ... as soon as ... thereafter` overstates the available day-level relation and conflicts with the explicit fallback rule.

3. **Trigger boundaries need one consistent policy.** The instruction to preserve a minimal event-bearing span conflicts with mechanical comma/period truncation in `upon, written notice`, section numbers, and multi-event clauses. The two DISAGREE challenge spans have straightforward shorter alternatives under the current policy. Other BORDERLINE rows need an explicit exception or a clearer gold-span convention; a rule that clips a section number or leaves an eventless `upon` should not be rewarded as better grounding merely because it is shorter.

4. **BLI pairing is source evidence, not just annotation metadata.** The inspected pairs establish the correct names and dates, but the converted anchor quotes contain only the value cell. Keep the pair or an explicit label/value evidence structure when testing the field-level invariant. A single date occurrence cannot, by itself, distinguish the Effective Date from the Commencement Date.

5. **A quote-scoped label must not inherit another clause's deadline.** I agree with the untimed labels for reference 6 and 38: the full segments contain an interest interval and immediate surrender respectively, but those words are outside the selected late-charge and termination fragments. Likewise, reference 34 must not borrow the surrounding ten-day cure period, challenge 13 must not import the cure period behind Section 16.1.1, and reference 48/49 must not silently acquire the thirty-day periods in separate defined-term clauses. Challenge 15 correctly ignores the surrounding observation that revenue commonly arrives sixty days after curtailment: the quoted obligation specifies the receipt month, not a guaranteed receipt day.

6. **Entitlement, eligibility and payment timing must stay distinct.** An outage-credit entitlement can be triggered by a qualifying interruption without making cash payable on that calendar day. In reference 46, the 168 hours qualify the loss event and are not an offset after it. The audit-reimbursement and optional-termination rows need a consistent decision about whether `contingent` includes an eligibility condition with no quoted performance interval; do not promote such a condition to an exact deadline.

The judgments above are label-review findings, not a recommendation to broaden model use or spend API budget. The remaining decisions belong in coordinator adjudication and the disclosed gold/grammar contract.
