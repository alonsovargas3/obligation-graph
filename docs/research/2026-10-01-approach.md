## What we skip

- **WFGY** (github.com/onestardao/WFGY). It's a pasted prompt protocol. It has no baseline benchmarks, no independent replication, and the engine is unreleased. Our trust comes from deterministic checks between steps, not from steering free-text reasoning, and an opaque overlay adds a variable we can't measure. Its 16-item RAG "Problem Map" is a reasonable checklist, but we have no retrieval step. Recorded in ADR-006.
- **Fuzzy or embedding grounding, LLM-as-judge verification.** These break the invariant.
- **Anthropic Citations API as the grounding authority.** It can't be combined with structured outputs (400 error), it cites at sentence granularity, and it proves a span exists, not that it supports the obligation. It may still be useful as an optional cross-check in the diff step if ablations leave a gap.
- **Fine-tuning and "domain small model" claims.** Non-goal.

## Anti-drift without an overlay

Drift is controlled at step boundaries, not in prompts:
1. Every step writes a typed artifact (text JSON, tool-call JSON, rows).
2. A deterministic checker gates each boundary (offset invariants after ingest, grounding after extract, FK and CHECK constraints at store, the `visible_obligation` view at read).
3. Drift is measured: run extraction 5 times on the gold doc and report run-to-run variance alongside precision and recall. Adopt a prompt change only if it beats baseline by more than that variance.

## Lacuna analysis

**1. Map the field.**
- Pre-signature drafting and negotiation: Spellbook, Robin AI, Luminance, Harvey drafting.
- Generative research and Q&A with citations: Harvey, Legora, CoCounsel, Lexis+ AI.
- Post-signature repository extraction: Kira, Evisort, Ironclad obligation extraction, Icertis, Sirion.
- Grid analytics: Hebbia Matrix.
- Academic per-document classification and NLI: CUAD, ContractNLI, MAUD, ACORD.

There are two dense clusters, drafting/negotiation and static repository extraction. The empty air is post-signature and dynamic.

**2. Hidden axis.** The field optimizes fidelity to *a document*. It counts success when a clause is found in a document. The customer counts failure when the extracted term is correct but no longer operative: superseded by amendment 3, overridden by a recognition agreement, or relative to a commencement date nobody resolved. Benchmarks (CUAD, BigLaw Bench) are per-document, so the axis is invisible to them.

**3. On-grid lacuna.** The *operative state* of a deal: obligations as amended, across the full document family, with lineage. The supersedes edges already point here. This is still a cell on the known grid. Law firms produce manual conformed copies, and CLMs offer parent/child document links. Expect traps.

**4. Force keeping it empty.**
- *Unrepresentable.* CLMs store documents as units and fields per document. Amendments reference sections by number ("Section 4.2 is deleted and replaced"), so resolving them needs cross-document reference resolution that per-document schemas can't express.
- *Liability.* Saying "this is the operative term" reads as legal interpretation.

**5. Decay test.**
- The representational force broke between 2024 and 2025. Long-context models (a full lease plus amendments fits in one context) and reliable tool-schema output made cross-document resolution cheap.
- The liability force still stands. Route around it: never assert "operative". Show the chain with citations and flag, the way the change-order report already does.

That makes this a door, not a wall.

**6. Corpses.**
- The graveyard is upstream: computable-contract efforts (Accord Project / Clause.io, Symboleo-style DSLs) that needed authors to draft in new formats. They died on adoption, not on the idea.
- Downstream derived graphs have few public corpses.
- Quiet internal CLM attempts are invisible to us.

Read: hard-not-forbidden, and under-discovered downstream.

**7. Missing axis: coupling between agreements.** The field treats the contract as the unit of analysis. The risk lives in the edges: a guarantee backstops a lease, a recognition agreement gives a third party step-in rights on default, and cross-defaults chain deals together. Finance measures counterparty-exposure networks as first-class. Contract AI does not measure them at all. The anchor document (a recognition agreement among landlord, tenant, and guarantor) is literally one of these edges.

**8. Fill.**
- *On-grid:* obligations carry a computed lifecycle and full supersession lineage. Every edge is cited. Relative deadlines resolve through anchor events instead of showing up as null due dates that silently fall out of `upcoming_deadlines`.
- *Missing axis:* a `triggers` edge type and one MCP query: "if obligation X is breached, what is triggered, owed by whom, and by when?" Every hop is cited.
- *Conviction:*
  - Lacuna diagnosis: medium-high (dense evidence from benchmark design and product patterns).
  - On-grid fill: high (cheap, falls out of the existing graph).
  - Missing-axis diagnosis: medium.
  - Missing-axis fill: medium. The value is real, but extraction of trigger edges is harder and the eval set is small. Keep it to one table and one tool; don't build a subsystem.

## Practical gaps found while doing this

- **"Form of" exhibits.** The anchor is a *form* of agreement with placeholders. Blanks get status `blank` and are never inferred.
- **Relative deadlines.** Without anchor events, "what do we owe in 90 days" returns nothing for most lease obligations.
- **Per-agreement roles.** A party is landlord in one agreement and counterparty in another. Role lives on `AgreementParty`.
- **Uncited edges.** Supersedes, guarantees, and triggers each need their own ClauseRef, or the graph states terms it can't point to.
- **Curly quotes and NBSP.** Grounding normalizes whitespace only (NBSP is whitespace). Quote characters are not normalized. Span selection makes this moot because text is copied from source.
